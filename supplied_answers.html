How does light travel through space?

Exam answer

Light travels through space as a transverse electromagnetic wave. It consists of mutually perpendicular electric and magnetic fields, both perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

Light does not require a material medium and can travel through vacuum. In vacuum, its speed is

c = 1 / √(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹

Conclusion: Light is an electromagnetic wave and travels through the vacuum of space.

Remember: E ⟂ B ⟂ direction of travel.

Write down the physical significance of Maxwell’s equations.

Exam answer

  1. Gauss’s law for electricity: electric charges produce electric fields.
  2. Gauss’s law for magnetism: isolated magnetic poles do not exist; magnetic field lines are closed.
  3. Faraday’s law: a changing magnetic field produces an electric field.
  4. Ampère–Maxwell law: electric current and a changing electric field produce a magnetic field.
c = 1 / √(μ₀ε₀) = 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹

Therefore, Maxwell’s equations unify electricity and magnetism and prove that light is an electromagnetic wave. They also explain electromagnetic phenomena such as radio, television, interference, diffraction and polarization.

Remember: charge → E, current → B, changing E ↔ changing B.

What are the conditions for interference?

Exam answer

The conditions for clear and steady interference are:

  1. The sources must be coherent, having the same frequency and a constant phase difference.
  2. The waves must be of the same nature and must overlap in the observation region.
  3. For light, the waves should have the same polarization and comparable amplitudes.
Bright: Δ = mλ   |   Dark: Δ = (m + ½)λ
Remember: coherent + overlapping + same polarization.

Prove that fringe width does not depend on fringe number.

Proof

Let slit separation be d, screen distance be D, wavelength be λ, and let the position of the nth bright fringe be yₙ.

For a bright fringe,

d sin θₙ = nλ

For small angles, sin θₙ ≈ yₙ/D. Therefore,

yₙ = nλD/d

The fringe width is the distance between consecutive bright fringes:

β = yₙ₊₁ − yₙ
= (n+1)λD/d − nλD/d
= λD/d

The fringe number n has disappeared. Therefore, fringe width is constant and does not depend on fringe number.

Remember: subtract consecutive positions; n cancels.

SPRING–2025 · 03 MARKS

Newton’s rings: find the radius of curvature.

Solution

Given:

  • Ring order, n = 10
  • Diameter, D₁₀ = 0.5 cm
  • Wavelength, λ = 5.9 × 10⁻⁵ cm

For the nth dark ring in reflected light:

Dₙ² = 4nλR

Therefore,

R = D₁₀² / (4nλ)
= (0.5)² / [4 × 10 × 5.9 × 10⁻⁵] cm
≈ 105.9 cm

Answer: R ≈ 106 cm ≈ 1.06 m.

Remember: square the diameter before substituting.
